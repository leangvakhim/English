Mastering Conjunctions

The Glue of English Grammar

What is a Conjunction?

Meaning: A conjunction is a word used to connect other words, phrases, or clauses together. They are the "joiners" that help us communicate complex ideas smoothly.

Without conjunctions, we would have to speak in short, choppy sentences.

Without Conjunctions:

I like cooking. I like eating. I don't like washing dishes.

With Conjunctions:

I like cooking and eating, but I don't like washing dishes.

1. Coordinating Conjunctions

Meaning: These join words, phrases, or independent clauses of equal grammatical rank or importance.

The FANBOYS Acronym:

F - For A - And N - Nor B - But O - Or Y - Yet S - So

Examples in Action:

  • Joining Words: She bought apples and bananas.

    Connects two nouns.

  • Joining Independent Clauses: I wanted to go for a walk, but it started to rain.

    Notice the comma before 'but' when joining two complete sentences.

2. Correlative Conjunctions

Meaning: These conjunctions always come in pairs. They work together to connect grammatically equal elements in a sentence.

Common Pairs:

Both ... And
Either ... Or
Neither ... Nor
Not only ... But also

Pair 2

Element A and Element B must be grammatically parallel (e.g., noun + noun).

Examples in Action:

  • I will eat either pizza or pasta for dinner.

    Joining two nouns.

  • She is not only smart but also hardworking.

    Joining two adjectives. Notice how the sentence feels balanced.

3. Subordinating Conjunctions

Meaning: These connect an independent clause (a complete thought) to a dependent clause (an incomplete thought). They show a relationship like cause, time, or condition.

Common Subordinators:

Because Although Since If When While Unless

The conjunction makes the clause it attaches to dependent.

Examples in Action:

  • I stayed home because it was raining.

    Structure 1: No comma needed.

  • Although she was tired, she finished her homework.

    Structure 2: Dependent clause first, so a comma is required.

Quick Summary

Type Function Examples
Coordinating Joins equals (words, phrases, independent clauses). and, but, or, so (FANBOYS)
Correlative Works in pairs to join grammatically equal elements. either/or, both/and
Subordinating Joins dependent clause to independent clause. because, although, if, since
