What is a Conjunctive Adverb?

A conjunctive adverb is an adverb that connects two independent clauses (complete thoughts). It provides a smooth transition and shows the relationship between the two clauses.

The Golden Rule of Punctuation:

When connecting two independent clauses, always use a semicolon (;) before the conjunctive adverb and a comma (,) after it.

Cause, Effect & Result

Meaning & Use

Used when the second clause is a direct result, consequence, or effect of the first clause.

Common Words

Contrast & Comparison

Meaning & Use

Used to show that the second clause contradicts, contrasts with, or presents an alternative to the first clause.

Common Words

Addition & Continuation

Meaning & Use
