Mastering Relative Pronouns

A step-by-step guide to connecting your sentences.

What is a Relative Pronoun?

A relative pronoun introduces a dependent (relative) clause and connects it to an independent clause. It essentially acts as a bridge, giving more information about a noun mentioned previously.

The Basic Structure

Independent Clause I met a man
Relative Pronoun who
Relative Clause lives in Spain.

1. Core

The essential connectors (who, whom, whose, which, that).

2. Compound

Pronouns combined with "-ever" (whoever, whatever).

3. Adverbs

Connecting based on time, place, or reason (when, where, why).

Relative (Adjective) Clauses

A relative clause (also called an adjective clause) is a dependent clause that modifies a noun or pronoun. It acts exactly like a giant adjective, and it is usually introduced by a relative pronoun or adverb!

How it works

The car
that is parked outside Adjective Clause
belongs to me.
The clause "that is parked outside" describes the noun "The car".
No Commas

Defining (Restrictive)

Provides essential information needed to identify the noun. The sentence changes meaning without it.

"Students who study hard get good grades."

(Tells us exactly *which* students)

Uses Commas

Non-Defining (Non-Restrictive)

Provides extra, non-essential information. The sentence still makes sense without it.

"My brother, who lives in Paris, is a chef."

(I only have one brother; location is extra info)

Core Relative Pronouns

These are the most common relative pronouns. Their usage depends on whether they refer to people or things, and their grammatical role (subject, object, or possessive) in the relative clause.

Who (Subject / People)

The student who studied hard got an A.

Which (Subject/Object / Things)

The book which I read was excellent.

Whose (Possessive)

I met a girl whose mother is a doctor.

That (Informal / People or Things)

Where is the car that you bought?

Compound Relative Pronouns

Formed by adding "-ever" or "-soever" to relative pronouns. They are more general and mean "any person who," "any thing which," etc. They do not refer to a specific noun antecedent.

who / whom / which / what

(Subject)

Meaning

Any person who.

Example

"Whoever finishes first wins a prize."

Whomever
(Object)

Meaning

Any person whom.

Example

"You can invite whomever you like."

Whatever / Whichever

Meaning

Anything that / Any one that (from a choice).

Example

"Take whatever you need from the fridge."

"Choose whichever flavor you prefer."

Relative Adverbs

Relative adverbs connect clauses just like relative pronouns, but they refer to a time, place, or reason. They can often replace a relative pronoun combined with a preposition (e.g., *where* = *in/at which*).

Where

Refers to a Place

"This is the house where I grew up."

= in which I grew up

When

Refers to a Time

"I remember the day when we met."

= on which we met

Why

Refers to a Reason

"Tell me the reason why you are late."

= for which you are late
